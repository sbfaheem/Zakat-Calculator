Islamic Zakat Principles

1. The Principle of Nisab (Obligation Threshold)

Zakat is only due if your total net wealth exceeds the Nisab threshold. The standard is calculated as the market price of either 87.48 grams of pure Gold or 612.36 grams of pure Silver. If your net wealth is below the threshold, no Zakat is due, but voluntary charity (Sadaqah) is highly encouraged.

2. The Principle of Al-Hawl (Lunar Year Possession)

The assets must remain in your possession for one complete lunar year (354 days) to qualify for Zakat. If your wealth fluctuates but stays above the Nisab standard during the year, Zakat is due at your calculation date. Citing Sunan Ibn Majah: "No Zakat is due on wealth until a year has passed over it."

3. Rate of Zakat (2.5%)

The Zakat due is calculated at exactly 2.5% (1/40th) of your total net assets. This rate applies specifically to monetary wealth, business merchandise, shares, and gold/silver.

4. Deductible Liabilities

According to Islamic jurisprudence, you may subtract outstanding debts due immediately or within the current lunar year from your assets. Long-term parts of mortgage payments or future interest are typically not deductible.

5. The Eight Recipients (Surah At-Tawbah 9:60)

Zakat cannot be spent on building mosques or public infrastructure. It must be paid to the eight groups of people ordained in the Quran: the poor, the needy, those employed to collect it, those whose hearts are to be reconciled, for freeing captives, those in debt, in the cause of Allah, and for the stranded traveler.

Nisab Threshold Details

The term Nisab defines the minimum wealth a Muslim must own for a full lunar year before Zakat becomes obligatory.

Formula Calculations

  • • Gold standard Nisab: 87.48 grams (7.5 Tolas)

Since silver price is lower, it reduces the threshold, allowing more individuals to contribute and maximizing the assistance provided to the poor and needy.

Who Can Receive My Zakat?

The 8 Quranic Categories (Surah At-Tawbah 9:60)

"Zakat expenditures are only for the poor and for the needy and for those employed to collect it and for bringing hearts together and for freeing captives and for those in debt and for the cause of Allah and for the [stranded] traveler — an obligation ordained by Allah. And Allah is Knowing and Wise."

— Surah At-Tawbah 9:60
🍞 1. The Poor (Al-Fuqara)

Families who have virtually no income or wealth, and cannot afford basic food, clothing, or shelter.

💼 2. The Needy (Al-Masakin)

People who work or have a small income, but it does not meet basic essential family costs and they possess less than Nisab.

📋 3. Zakat Collectors (Al-Amilina Alayha)

Trustworthy workers authorized by official or community bodies to collect, account for, and distribute Zakat funds.

🤝 4. Reconciling Hearts (Al-Mu'allafatu Qulubuhum)

New Muslims in need of community welcome, encouragement, and relief from economic hardship.

⛓️ 5. Freeing Captives (Fir-Riqab)

Freeing people from human trafficking, bonded labor exploitation, or unjust captivity.

🧾 6. Burdened Debtors (Al-Gharimin)

People burdened by overwhelming debts incurred for basic survival, medical emergencies, or disasters, with no means to repay.

📖 7. In Allah's Cause (Fi Sabilillah)

Those engaged in the advancement of religious knowledge, Islamic scholars/students, and community welfare striving in faith.

🧳 8. The Stranded Traveler (Ibn As-Sabil)

Anyone stranded on a lawful journey without access to their funds to safely return home, even if wealthy in their home country.

🚫 Who CANNOT Receive Your Zakat:
  • Direct ascendants: Your parents, grandparents, and great-grandparents (you must care for them directly).
  • Direct descendants: Your children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren.
  • Your spouse: Husband or wife cannot pay Zakat to one another.
  • Wealthy individuals: Anyone whose net wealth equals or exceeds the Nisab threshold.
  • Public infrastructure: Building mosques, digging wells for public roads, or general charities where ownership is not transferred directly to an eligible person.
